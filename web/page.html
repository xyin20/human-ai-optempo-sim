Companion to the lightning talk · Measuring mutual adaptation as one human–AI system

Human–AI Teaming

Four people wear nine physiological sensors. A tiny language model reads their radio chat and answers. A pooled analysis turns everyone’s signals into team states and asks one question: when do the people and the AI start behaving like one system?

Everything on this page is simulated. The human signals come from a model of workload and engagement; the AI signals come from real forward passes of a 125k-parameter transformer on the simulated chat. Coupling strengths were chosen to demonstrate the pipeline, so the numbers are not empirical findings.

1

The session

Four teammates, P1 to P4, share one radio channel for a 60-minute mission. Targets appear at random; people report them, request action, confirm and adjust, and the AI teammate answers requests. Under load, people send more messages, add urgency, and slip from short brevity codes into vague free-form text. That is the path from a person’s state into the AI.

2

Two kinds of signals

Each person has nine physiological sensors sampled at 1 Hz: interbeat interval, respiration and pupil (autonomic), and EEG delta, theta, alpha, beta plus fNIRS HbO and HbR (neural). The AI gets its own sensors, read from the model every second: what each of its eight attention heads is doing, and how surprised, uncertain and busy the model is.

4

Symbolize, pool, concatenate

Each member’s signal becomes a quartile symbol (0–3) within that member’s own distribution, so “high” means high for that person. Next, signals are pooled across members within each sensor. The people are pooled across the four humans; the AI is pooled across its eight attention heads, which play the part of members. The pooled sensor values are concatenated into one state label for the team, one for the AI, and one for the whole human–AI system.

How many distinct states?

Concatenating every raw symbol makes almost every second a new state, so short-window entropy sits at its ceiling and cannot move. Pooling first keeps states that recur.

5

Three pools, one system

The analysis computes Shannon entropy of the pooled states in a sliding window, smooths it over 200 s, and drops the first 159 s. Doing that for the people, the AI and the concatenated system gives three entropies. If the people and the AI were independent, the system’s entropy would equal the sum of the two parts. The shortfall is the information they share:

I = H(people) + H(AI) − H(people ⊕ AI)

Finite samples inflate that number, so each estimate is compared with surrogates that circularly shift the AI’s stream within the phase. That keeps each stream’s own rhythm and breaks only their timing together.

Over time · 10-minute sliding window

The coupling in this simulation is slow, so it shows best in long windows. The 30-second windows used for entropy pick up mostly fast fluctuations.

6

A person’s signals carry the system

Take one person’s own autonomic state (interbeat interval, respiration, pupil: high or low) and compare it with the AI’s pooled output state. None of that person’s data is in the AI’s stream. Any shared information above chance means the person’s physiology is carrying part of the AI’s state, and the other way round.

Where the AI’s attention goes

The AI’s attention is itself a reading of each person: it turns on when the AI joins this team’s channel and grows as people talk to it.

7

What this means for measuring mutual adaptation

The systematic review defines system-level mutual adaptation as reciprocal, dynamic and emergent change in the human–AI system that cannot be attributed to either agent alone, unfolding at several levels. Each part of that definition maps to something this pipeline can measure.

EmergentI = H(people) + H(AI) − H(system) above surrogate

Shared information is a property of the pair. Neither stream contains it alone.

Dynamicwindowed entropy and integration over time

The measures move as coupling switches on, so adaptation can be tracked within a session.

Multilevelneural ↔ attention, autonomic ↔ output

Layer pairs show where the coupling lives. Here it is clearest in autonomic ↔ output.

Reciprocalnext step: lagged AMI, transfer entropy

Shared information is symmetric. It shows coupling but not its direction, so one-way and mutual phases look alike here.

Limits

Simulated data with chosen coupling strengths; the demo shows that the pipeline can see coupling, not how strong it is in real teams.

Mutual information is statistical dependence. It is not causal influence or information flow.

The pooling rule and the window length change the answer, so both should be treated as a sensitivity analysis.
